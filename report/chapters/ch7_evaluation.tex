\chapter{Evaluation}
\label{ch:evaluation}

The evaluation combines four complementary methods, chosen to match what each part
of the system actually claims. Trained models are evaluated with standard detection
metrics on held-out data. The voice front-end is evaluated offline against a
constructed utterance corpus. Decision logic --- everything the user hears, and
when --- is verified by automated behavioral tests that replay scripted scenarios
deterministically. The full perception stack, which no offline metric can certify
against real rooms, real lighting, and real clutter, was evaluated through
iterative live trials on phones that directly shaped the design. We report each in
turn, then trace the outcome back to the requirements of
Chapter~\ref{ch:system_design}, and close with the system's operational
performance profile.

Precision of $0.968$ --- very few false doors --- is the metric that matters most
for safety, and it is materially helped by the 827 hard-negative images (handles,
cabinets) included in training. Recall of $0.892$ means roughly one door in ten is
missed in a single frame; the system's temporal accumulation across the room scan
makes the practical miss rate far lower. At the navigation loop's 3\,Hz, a slow
full turn exposes the detector to on the order of a hundred frames per room, and a
door needs only three sighting units across the entire sweep --- two frames if the
verifier corroborates them --- to become a target. Under an independence
approximation, even a per-frame recall well below the validation figure leaves the
probability of missing a door that stays in view for a handful of frames
vanishingly small; conversely, the same accumulation is why per-frame
\emph{precision} is the number the design leans on, since false positives are the
error the funnel exists to absorb. The mAP@50 of $0.946$ comfortably exceeds the
${\geq}0.6$ target set when the milestone was planned.

\emph{An important caveat we report deliberately:} the validation images come from
the same public datasets as training, so these numbers measure generalization
across images, not across domains. The live trials
(Section~\ref{sec:live_trials}) exposed domain-shift failures --- most notably
textured walls scoring as doors at high confidence --- that the validation set
cannot, which is precisely why the perception funnel of
Section~\ref{sec:door_funnel} exists: the deployed system never acts on the raw
detector alone.

\section{Offline Evaluation of the Command Parser}
\label{sec:parser_eval}

The command parser is the system's single point of entry --- every task begins
with it --- so it was evaluated offline against a constructed corpus of
\textbf{533 utterances}, run through the committed parser implementation. The
corpus was built systematically rather than sampled, in six blocks: the exhaustive
grid of every supported noun under every Object Allocation prefix
($21\times16=336$); the exhaustive grid of every destination under every
navigation prefix ($10\times10=100$); 25 noisy utterances combining
transcription-style mishearings (``kichen'', ``botle'', ``labtop'',
``refrigirator''), synonyms (``washroom'', ``lounge'', ``study'', ``sofa''),
spacing errors (``bed room''), and trailing filler; the full completion vocabulary
plus filler-wrapped variants (28); the full information-query vocabulary (24);
and 20 out-of-scope utterances (``what time is it'', ``play some music'', ``set a
timer'') that the parser must classify as \emph{unknown} rather than force into a
task. An utterance counts as correct only if both the task type and the canonical
target are exact. Table~\ref{tab:parser_eval} reports the results.

The three failures are as informative as the successes. ``Find my cup, thanks''
was classified as a \emph{completion} --- the trailing ``thanks'' is itself a
confirm phrase, and the token-set matcher scores it above the completion
threshold on a five-word utterance; the guard rails (short-utterance limit, high
threshold) contain the pattern but do not eliminate it when a confirm word rides
on a command. A self-correction (``I need my glasses, no wait, my cup'') exceeded
the tolerable edit distance for any single split and fell to \emph{unknown} ---
the safe failure, since the user is re-prompted rather than sent after the wrong
object. Finally, one out-of-scope utterance (``read me the news'') was
force-matched to a navigation command through the permissive prefix threshold ---
the known cost of thresholds tuned to favor recall over precision at the task
layer, where a wrong confirmation is immediately audible and correctable. The
exhaustive clean grids passing in full confirm the property the test suite also
asserts: every supported noun and destination is reachable through every
template.

\section{Automated Behavioral Verification}
\label{sec:behavioral_eval}

The automated suite (Section~\ref{sec:test_infrastructure}) functions as a
behavioral specification: each test asserts that a scripted situation produces the
required action and utterance. On the submitted revision the suite passes at
\textbf{281/281}, completing in \textbf{0.7\,s} with no ML model loaded; the
per-module composition is given in Table~\ref{tab:tests}. Highlights relevant to
the system's claims:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Complete navigation journeys.} A scripted two-room journey drives
        the controller through a full compass scan (36 simulated headings), door
        selection from clustered bearings, the guided turn to face it, the
        one-shot door call-out followed by the path-clear verdict, the at-door
        instruction, doorway transit --- asserted to require both door
        disappearance and sustained camera motion --- the new room's scan
        anchored to the new heading, indicator sighting, directed confirmation,
        and the arrival announcement naming the observed evidence (``fridge''),
        with the exact mode transitions and key phrases asserted at every step.
  \item \textbf{Safety invariants.} Tests assert that a blocking obstacle
        overrides door guidance with a priority warning; that an empty room
        produces a rescan rather than a fabricated target; that a lone sink never
        declares a kitchen while a refrigerator does (the primary/secondary
        arrival rule, exercised across all six destination types); and that
        arrival evidence must be localized --- scan-state resets between rooms
        are verified so evidence can never leak across a doorway.
  \item \textbf{FSM discipline.} All valid transitions, all rejections (e.g.,
        \texttt{task\_complete} outside an active task), the no-op semantics of
        unrecognized commands, subscriber notification exactly on real
        transitions, and the abort path that returns to listening rather than
        idle --- so a voice ``cancel'' never strands the user at a Start button
        they cannot see.
  \item \textbf{Voice robustness.} The command parser is tested against 50+
        realistic transcriptions, including mishearings (``cop'' $\to$ cup,
        ``fone'' $\to$ cell phone, ``microvave'' $\to$ microwave), synonyms
        (``sofa'', ``fridge'', ``washroom''), trailing filler, punctuation and
        case noise, multi-word targets (``dining table'', ``living room''), and
        the completion, cancel, and information vocabularies used mid-task; a
        closing pair of tests verifies that \emph{every} supported noun and
        destination is reachable through at least one template.
  \item \textbf{Reach guidance geometry.} The touch condition at box corners and
        edges, the dominant-axis rule for diagonal offsets, the almost-zone
        threshold, and defensive behavior on degenerate frames and off-frame
        fingertips.
  \item \textbf{Isolation.} Concurrent sessions are verified to share no
        navigation state, and re-entering a room-scan phase is verified to reset
        exactly the per-room evidence.
\end{itemize}

Because the decision layer is pure, these are not smoke tests: they replay the
same code paths the live system executes, with only the perception inputs faked at
the same interfaces the engines use.

\section{Live Trials}
\label{sec:live_trials}

The perception stack was evaluated the only way it can be: on phones, in real
multi-room residential settings, across furniture, lighting, and clutter. These
trials ran continuously throughout development, and their findings are the origin
of nearly every mechanism in Chapter~\ref{ch:task_families}.

\subsection{Methodology}

Trials used sighted testers executing full task sessions on real phones over an
HTTPS tunnel to the development server. The perception pipeline prints a
per-candidate decision trace --- every door candidate's confidence, edge density,
corroboration status, and keep/reject verdict, every distance estimate with its
inputs, every obstacle-lane verdict --- so each failure could be diagnosed from
logs, addressed by a threshold change or a structural mechanism, and re-tested in
the same rooms. The navigation pipeline completed full two-room journeys --- scan,
door approach, obstacle interruption, doorway transit, second scan, semantic
arrival --- end-to-end on a phone, first as the standalone prototype and again
after its integration into the final backend.

\subsection{Qualitative Outcomes}

Beyond the individual fixes, three end-to-end behaviors were established in live
runs and are worth stating as results in their own right.

\textbf{Multi-room journeys complete.} The full loop --- guided scan, door
call-out, approach, doorway transit, fresh scan in the new room, semantic arrival
on the kitchen's fridge and oven --- runs end-to-end on a phone, two rooms deep,
producing the session transcript shown throughout
Chapter~\ref{ch:task_families}. The doorway transit, identified in the design
phase as the riskiest step, resolves reliably once gated on camera motion: the
system waits at the door while the user stands still and announces the transit
shortly after they actually walk.

\textbf{Obstacle interruption behaves as specified.} A chair placed in the walking
lane mid-approach produced the warning, the sidestep advice, the repeated prompt
while still blocked, the all-clear, and re-orientation toward the door --- in that
order, in merged utterances, without talking over itself. The floor-segmentation
layer flagged unnamed clutter (boxes, a standing fan, clothes piles --- none of
which COCO can name) when it lay in the walked corridor while staying silent about
the same objects beside it, which is the distinction the earlier depth-based
formulation failed to make.

\textbf{Semantic arrival is convincing and resistant to noise.} Entering a
kitchen, the scan names the refrigerator and oven it confirmed and declares
arrival; the localized-evidence rule visibly suppressed the false arrivals that
scattered single-frame detections previously caused, and the directed double-check
means a journey never ends on scan evidence the user was not shown.

The distance model deserves a specific note: after the portrait-axis correction
and the tighter-box measurement, spoken step counts matched paced-out distances
closely enough in trials that the one-shot calibration constant was left at its
neutral value.

\section{System Performance}
\label{sec:performance}

The system's responsiveness rests on a small number of measured and structural
facts rather than on raw model speed.

\textbf{Perception cadence and budget.} Object Allocation analyses frames at
5\,FPS, giving each tick a 200\,ms budget: one YOLOv8n inference (a ${\sim}6$\,MB
nano model at $640$ input, CPU real-time at this rate) plus, only in the reach
phase, one MediaPipe hand inference measured at ${\sim}15$\,ms per frame during
development --- which is precisely why the hand model is gated on the tracker's
\emph{near} verdict rather than run continuously. Navigation runs at 3\,FPS, a
333\,ms budget across up to four models per frame (YOLOv8m, the two door models,
and SegFormer during walking), with two structural savings: the COCO model is
class-filtered to the current goal's indicators (plus obstacle classes only while
walking), and the verifier runs only when there is a door candidate or a fridge
claim to check. All inference executes in worker threads, so the event loop ---
and with it the WebSocket, the voice pipeline, and other sessions --- never blocks
on a frame. Both loops read the \emph{latest} frame rather than a queue, so
processing can never fall progressively behind the camera: under load the system
skips frames, it does not lag. The motion gate adds a further saving during scans:
blurred frames are skipped before any model runs, at the cost of a $64\times48$
grayscale difference.

\textbf{Evidence timing.} The scan and confirmation constants translate into
concrete user-facing latencies at 3\,Hz. A door needs three sighting units ---
two verifier-corroborated frames, or three bare strong-confidence frames --- of
the roughly one hundred a slow full turn provides, so a real door is virtually
never missed by a completed scan, while a wall pattern must fool the funnel three
separate times to become a target. An indicator needs four localized sightings to
arm an arrival and three more during the directed re-check, plus a ${\sim}1$\,s
settle window --- so a confirmed arrival costs the user roughly two seconds of
steady pointing, while a scattered artifact can never accumulate the localized
evidence at all. The obstacle watchdog's 3-blocked / 2-clear debounce means a
warning follows a real blocker by ${\sim}1$\,s and an all-clear follows a sidestep
by ${\sim}0.7$\,s --- fast enough to precede the user's next step at walking pace,
slow enough that single-frame flicker never speaks.

\textbf{Startup and first-use costs.} The heavyweight navigation models load once
per server lifetime, in a worker thread, announced to the user (``Give me a
moment\ldots'') rather than endured as silence, and are warmed on a blank frame so
GPU initialization is not paid on the first real one. Every public model weight is
fetched and cached automatically on first use (Table~\ref{tab:models}); the custom
door weights ship in the repository, so a fresh clone reaches a working system
with two commands (Section~\ref{sec:deployment}).

\textbf{Speech latency.} Guidance phrases are templated, so the bounded 50-entry
TTS cache converts the recurring lines --- confirmations, scan instructions,
obstacle warnings --- into cache hits after first use, removing the synthesis
network round trip from the steady state. Synthesis for cache misses runs in a
worker thread; detection continues underneath it. On the client, the
gesture-primed persistent audio element plays clips back-to-back with no per-clip
setup, and the server-side ambient throttling (no ambient line within 2.5\,s of
any line, no identical ambient line within 8\,s) guarantees the audio queue cannot
build a backlog that lags behind reality --- the property that matters more to a
blind user than any single clip's latency.

\textbf{Verification cost.} The complete behavioral suite runs in 0.7\,s with no
models loaded, which in practice meant it ran on every change --- the property
that kept the pure decision layer correct while the perception layer was being
reworked failure-by-failure.
