\chapter{Evaluation}
\label{ch:evaluation}

The evaluation combines three complementary methods, chosen to match what each part
of the system actually claims. Trained models are evaluated with standard detection
metrics on held-out data. Decision logic --- everything the user hears, and when ---
is verified by automated behavioral tests that replay scripted scenarios
deterministically. The full perception stack, which no offline metric can certify
against real rooms, real lighting, and real hands, was evaluated through iterative
live trials that directly shaped the design. We report each in turn, then system
performance, and close with an honest account of limitations.

\label{sec:door_eval}

Precision of $0.968$ --- very few false doors --- is the metric that matters most
for safety, and it is materially helped by the 827 hard-negative images (handles,
cabinets) included in training. Recall of $0.892$ means roughly one door in ten is
missed in a single frame; the system's temporal accumulation across the room scan
makes the practical miss rate far lower, since a door need only be caught in three
frames of an entire sweep. The mAP@50 of $0.946$ comfortably exceeds the
${\geq}0.6$ target set when the milestone was planned.

\emph{An important caveat we report deliberately:} validation images come from the
same public datasets as training, so these numbers measure generalization across
images, not across domains. Live trials (Section~\ref{sec:live_trials}) exposed
domain-shift failures --- most notably textured walls --- that the validation set
cannot, which is precisely why the perception funnel exists.

\section{Automated Behavioral Verification}
\label{sec:behavioral_eval}

The 286-test suite (Section~\ref{sec:test_infrastructure}) functions as a
behavioral specification: each test asserts that a scripted situation produces the
required action and utterance. Highlights relevant to the system's claims:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Complete navigation journeys.} A scripted two-room journey drives
        the controller through a compass scan (36 simulated headings), door
        selection from clustered bearings, the guided turn, the door call-out and
        path-clear verdict, the at-door instruction, doorway transit (requiring
        both door disappearance and sustained camera motion), the new room's scan,
        indicator sighting, directed confirmation, and the arrival announcement
        naming the observed evidence --- asserting the exact mode transitions and
        key phrases at every step.
  \item \textbf{Safety invariants.} Tests assert that a blocking obstacle overrides
        door guidance; that transit can never fire without the movement gate; that
        a lone sink never declares a kitchen while a refrigerator does; that
        scattered detector noise cannot sum to an arrival (localization windows);
        and that the room-cap check-in appears at exactly the configured cadence.
  \item \textbf{Timing semantics.} Pacing is verified to be time-based rather than
        frame-based: reprompts respect their configured seconds under varying tick
        rates, and absence timers cannot be rushed by stalls.
  \item \textbf{Voice robustness.} The command parser is tested against 50+
        realistic transcriptions, including mishearings (``cop''$\to$cup,
        ``fone''$\to$phone), synonyms, filler words, and the completion/cancel
        vocabulary used mid-task.
  \item \textbf{Isolation.} Concurrent sessions are verified to share no
        navigation state.
\end{itemize}

The suite runs in under one second and passes at 286/286 on the submitted revision.

\section{Live Trials}
\label{sec:live_trials}

The perception stack was evaluated the only way it can be: on phones, in real
apartments, across furniture, lighting, and clutter. These trials were run
continuously during development; their findings are the origin of nearly every
mechanism in Chapter~\ref{ch:task_families}. We summarize the methodology and the
principal findings.

\subsection{Methodology}

Trials used sighted testers executing full task sessions on real phones over a
tunnel to the development server, in multi-room residential settings. Each failure
was diagnosed from logs (per-candidate perception traces) and addressed either by a
threshold change or a structural mechanism, then re-tested. The navigation
prototype completed full two-room journeys --- scan, door approach, obstacle
interruption, doorway transit, second scan, semantic arrival --- end-to-end on a
phone, repeatedly, before and after its integration into the final backend.

Two qualitative outcomes deserve mention. First, obstacle interruption behaves as
designed in live runs: a chair placed in the walking lane mid-approach produced the
warning, the sidestep advice, the all-clear, and re-orientation toward the door, in
that order, without talking over itself. Second, semantic arrival is convincing in
practice: entering a kitchen, the scan names the refrigerator and oven it confirmed
and declares arrival --- and the localized-evidence rule visibly suppressed the
false arrivals that scattered single-frame detections previously caused.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

We state limitations plainly; several are fundamental to the no-infrastructure
design bet rather than implementation gaps.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{No localization.} Without SLAM or maps, deep multi-room journeys
        rely on the room cap, the timeout, and the user's own coarse knowledge;
        the system does not remember which doors it has already tried, so a
        dead-end branch can be re-entered.
  \item \textbf{Direction hints are not yet conversational.} When several doors
        are visible, the system auto-selects (most-sighted, then straight-ahead)
        rather than asking ``which way?'' and parsing the answer --- the designed
        M3/M4 interaction remains future work.
  \item \textbf{Domain shift can defeat the funnel.} In some rooms, textured walls
        fool all three door models simultaneously; the gates contain the damage,
        but the durable fix is a hard-negative fine-tune on exactly such footage.
  \item \textbf{Advisory, not collision-avoidance.} Guidance is 2--4\,m advance
        information at ${\sim}3$\,FPS; the white cane remains responsible for the
        contact zone, and trials kept a sighted spotter present.
  \item \textbf{Compass dependence.} The $360^\circ$ scan's bearings degrade with
        magnetic interference --- ironically strongest near the appliances that
        define a kitchen; the compass-less fallback loses bearing summaries.
  \item \textbf{Online TTS dependence.} Speech synthesis requires connectivity;
        the phrase cache and preload mitigate but do not remove this.
  \item \textbf{Single language; curated vocabularies.} English only; six
        destination room types; a curated object list.
  \item \textbf{No blind-user study yet.} All trials used sighted testers;
        conclusions about cognitive load and usability for the target population
        require a formal user study (Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion}).
\end{enumerate}
